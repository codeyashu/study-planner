## Day 1 — Grammar: Prepositions and collocations {#day-1}
**⏱ 30 min · Mon**

### Rule in 60 seconds
- Prepositions in English are largely **fixed by the word before them** (verb, adjective or noun). Learn them as chunks: *responsible for, depend on, interested in, access to, reason for, increase in.*
- **Time:** *at* + clock time or a point (at 10 a.m., at the end of the sprint); *on* + day or date (on Monday, on 3 October); *in* + month, year, period (in October, in Q4, in the morning). *By* = no later than; *until* = continuing up to.
- **Place/level:** *in* the cloud, *on* the server, *at* the office, *on* the roadmap, *in* the backlog.
- **Changes:** *an increase in* costs, *a reduction in* latency, *an investment in* tooling, *a demand for* skills, *the answer to* a question.
- **Verbs with fixed prepositions:** *depend on, rely on, consist of, result in / result from, agree with (a person) / on (a plan) / to (a proposal), apologize for, comply with, participate in, contribute to, focus on.*
- **Verbs with no preposition (transitive):** *discuss, emphasize, attend, reach, enter, answer (the question), mention, contact, address.*
- **The error fluent speakers make:** translating the preposition from their first language ("depend of", "discuss about", "responsible of", "interested on") and adding an unneeded one ("emphasize on", "reach to the office"). *Different from* is safe everywhere; *different than* is mainly American.

### Exercise
Choose the correct preposition (or "-" for none).

1. We are responsible ___ the deployment pipeline. (for / of)
2. The outcome depends ___ the vendor's response. (on / of)
3. She is very good ___ estimating effort. (at / in)
4. Please reply ___ the customer before noon. (to / -)
5. The new service is similar ___ the old one in design. (to / with)
6. We agreed ___ a new timeline yesterday. (on / with)
7. The team should invest ___ observability. (in / on)
8. Access ___ production is restricted to on-call engineers. (to / for)
9. The change resulted ___ a 20 percent drop in errors. (in / from)
10. The review is ___ Monday ___ 10 a.m. ___ the second week of October. (on/at/in, in/on/at, at/in/on)

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **for** (*responsible for*). 2. **on** (*depend on*). 3. **at** (*good at* + gerund). 4. **to** (*reply to*; *answer* takes none). 5. **to** (*similar to*). 6. **on** (*agree on* a plan or decision; *agree with* a person). 7. **in** (*invest in*). 8. **to** (*access to*). 9. **in** (*result in* = cause; *result from* = be caused by). 10. **on Monday at 10 a.m. in the second week** (day / clock time / period).

    Trap in item 9: "resulted from" would reverse cause and effect: "A 20 percent drop resulted from the change."

### Use it
Write three sentences about your work using *depend on*, *responsible for* and *a reduction in*. Check each preposition against a learner dictionary (Cambridge or Oxford Learner's).

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: Word nuance: affect/impact/influence, ensure/assure/insure {#day-2}
**⏱ 30 min · Tue**

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| affect | verb | to change or have an effect on something (verb; the noun is *effect*) | The new rate limit will affect every partner integration. |
| impact | noun / verb | noun: a strong effect. Verb use ("impacts our roadmap") is standard in business English; some writers still prefer *affect* or *have an impact on* | The outage had a serious impact on booking volume. |
| ensure | verb | to make certain that something happens or is true | We run canary releases to ensure a failed deployment never reaches all users. |
| assure | verb | to tell someone confidently that something is true, to remove their doubt (the object is a person) | I can assure you that customer data was not exposed. |
| insure | verb | to protect with an insurance policy (in both US and UK usage; *ensure* is for certainty) | The shipper insured the container for its full value. |

### Collocations and word family
- **affect** vs **effect**: *affect* (verb) = change; *effect* (noun) = the result: "The change affected latency; the effect was small." (*Effect* as a verb = to bring about: "effect change", formal.)
- **have an impact on / a positive impact / a significant impact / measure the impact / impact analysis**. *Influence*: **influence a decision / have influence over / exert influence / influence on**. *Influence* implies indirect persuasion; *affect* is a factual change; *impact* stresses size.
- **ensure** + that-clause or noun: *ensure compliance / ensure that...*; **assure** + person: *assure customers / assure someone that...*; noun *assurance* (quality assurance, "give assurances").
- **insure** against risk; *insurance*, *insurer*, *underinsured*. Some US style guides accept *insure* for "ensure" but avoid it.

### Exercise
Cloze: choose the best word.

1. The migration will ___ how we handle every customer's data. (affect / effect)
2. We added checks to ___ that no invalid records enter the warehouse. (ensure / assure)
3. Please ___ the client that we are treating this as a priority. (ensure / assure)
4. The cargo was ___ against loss at sea. (insured / ensured)
5. The latency spike had a serious ___ on conversion. (impact / affect)
6. The audit had no ___ on our release date. (effect / affect)
7. Senior engineers ___ decisions even without formal authority. (influence / ensure)
8. Which is a noun: affect, effect (in everyday business use)?

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. affect (verb). 2. ensure (make certain). 3. assure (object is a person: the client). 4. insured (policy). 5. impact (noun, after *serious*). 6. effect (noun after *no*). 7. influence (persuade indirectly). 8. *effect* (*affect* as a noun exists in psychology only).

### Use it
Write three sentences about a change you made recently, using *affect*, *ensure* and *assure*. Say them aloud.

## Day 3 — Speaking: Word stress and sentence rhythm {#day-3}
**⏱ 30 min · Wed**

### Prompt (60-90 seconds)
"Explain to a new team member how we handle deployments and what could go wrong. Use the words *process, production, approval, rollback, monitoring*."

### Structure
Say the process as a sequence with signposts ("First..., then..., finally..."), then one thing that could go wrong.

### Pronunciation micro-drill
English is **stress-timed**: content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs, negatives) get stress; function words (the, of, to, and, is) are short and weak. Beats stay roughly evenly spaced; the unstressed words squeeze in between.

- Practice: "**DE**ploy to **STAG**ing, **RUN** the **TESTS**, and **AP**prove the **RE**lease." (Tap on the capitals; keep the small words fast.)
- Word stress that changes meaning: **RE-cord** (noun) / **re-CORD** (verb); **PRO-cess** (noun) / **pro-CEED**; **OB-ject** / **ob-JECT**; **PER-mit** / **per-MIT**.
- Word stress in your everyday vocabulary: **de-VEL-op, de-VEL-op-ment** (same stress in both), **e-CO-no-my, e-co-NOM-ic, INF-ra-struc-ture, ar-chi-TEC-ture, a-VAIL-a-BIL-i-ty**.
- Suffixes shift stress: *-tion, -ic, -ity, -ical* put stress on the syllable *before* the suffix: **con-fig-u-RA-tion, ex-pla-NA-tion, pro-duc-TIV-i-ty**.
- Weak forms: "for" /fə/, "to" /tə/, "and" /ən/, "of" /əv/: "a lot of bugs" sounds like "a LOTta BUGS".

## Day 4 — Idioms & phrasal verbs: Progress and delay: behind schedule, move the needle {#day-4}
**⏱ 30 min · Thu**

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| behind schedule | later than planned; neutral, common in all registers | The integration is two weeks behind schedule because of vendor delays. |
| move the needle | make a measurable difference; informal, US business | Renaming variables won't move the needle; fixing the cache miss rate will. |
| on track | progressing as planned; neutral | The migration is on track for the 30 November cutover. |
| slip through the cracks | be overlooked or forgotten (a task or a person); informal, common | The security fix slipped through the cracks during the handover. |
| hit a snag | run into an unexpected small problem; informal, common in UK and US | We hit a snag with certificate renewal but resolved it in an hour. |

### Exercise
1. Complete: "The project is ___ schedule, so we need to cut scope."
2. Which idiom describes work that makes a real, measurable difference?
3. "Nobody followed up, so the ticket ___ through the ___."
4. Choose: "We're on ___ / off ___ / at ___ for the deadline." (track/road/path)
5. Rewrite idiomatically: "We encountered a small unexpected problem during testing."
6. Rewrite plainly: "That optimization won't move the needle."
7. True or false: *hit a snag* suggests a serious, project-ending failure.
8. Which is more suitable in a formal report: "We hit a snag" or "We encountered an issue"?

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. behind. 2. move the needle. 3. slipped, cracks. 4. track (*on track*). 5. "We hit a snag during testing." 6. "That optimization won't make a noticeable difference." 7. False: a snag is minor. 8. "We encountered an issue" (more neutral; the idiom is fine in team chat).

!!! warning "When NOT to use them"
    *Slip through the cracks* implies someone failed: in a postmortem, use it carefully or describe the process gap instead. Do not use *move the needle* if you can name the metric; the number is stronger than the idiom.

## Day 5 — Writing: Clear status updates {#day-5}
**⏱ 30 min · Fri**

A good status update answers: **Where are we? (RAG: red/amber/green) What changed? What is at risk? What do you need?** Keep to the facts; use dates and numbers.

### Bad draft
> Hi all, quick update on the payment project. Things are going okay overall. We did a lot of work this week on the stuff we talked about and testing is happening. There were some problems but the team is working on it. It might be delayed a bit. We'll see. Let me know if you have questions.

### Task
Rewrite under 100 words, with: overall status (amber), what was completed, the main risk with a date impact, and one clear ask.

??? success "Model answer and what changed"
    > **Payment gateway: status AMBER (week of 6 Oct)**
    >
    > **Done:** API integration complete; 80 of 120 test cases passing.
    > **Risk:** 12 failing cases involve refunds. The vendor's fix is due 10 Oct; if late, go-live moves from 24 Oct to 31 Oct.
    > **Next:** finish remaining tests by 15 Oct.
    > **Ask:** Priya, please escalate to the vendor's account manager if the fix is not delivered by 10 Oct.

    - Status label (RAG) in the title.
    - Numbers replace "a lot of" and "some problems".
    - The risk states cause and date impact.
    - "We'll see" was replaced with a trigger date and an ask.
    - Structure (Done / Risk / Next / Ask) makes it skimmable.

## Day 6 — Speaking record: Word stress and sentence rhythm (2-minute recording) {#day-6}
**⏱ 30 min · Sat**

### Task
Record 2 minutes. **Prompt:** "Explain a delay that happened on one of your projects: what caused it, what you did, and what you would do differently." Focus on stress-timed rhythm and clear content-word stress.

### Structure
Situation (25 s) -> Cause (30 s) -> Actions (35 s) -> Lesson (30 s).

### Scoring rubric (1-4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | hard to follow | main idea recoverable | clear | effortless |
| Structure | no order | partial | clear start and end | signposted |
| Fluency | frequent long pauses | noticeable hesitations | occasional | smooth |
| Grammar accuracy | blocks meaning | frequent errors | occasional | rare |
| Vocabulary range | basic | some variety | good | precise, varied |
| Pronunciation | unintelligible in places | needs effort | mostly clear | clear stress and rhythm |

### Shadowing task
Shadow a clear speaker for 8 minutes: a BBC Learning English "6 Minute English" episode (bbc.co.uk/learningenglish) or a TED talk (ted.com). Copy the *rhythm* first, then the sounds.

**Log your score:** date, total /24, weakest criterion, three stress mistakes.

## Day 7 — Soft skills + weekly review: Giving feedback with SBI {#day-7}
**⏱ 30 min · Sun**

### Scenario
A senior engineer, Dana, joined your design review 15 minutes late and interrupted a junior engineer twice. The junior engineer stopped talking afterward. You are her lead and want to raise it privately.

### Task
Script your feedback (about 150 words) using **SBI**: *Situation* (when and where), *Behavior* (what you observed, no judgment), *Impact* (the effect on you, others or the work), then a question.

??? success "Model response and techniques"
    > "Dana, do you have five minutes? I wanted to talk about this morning's design review. When we were discussing the queue design (situation), I noticed you interrupted Sam twice while he was explaining the trade-offs (behavior). After that he stopped contributing, and we lost his view on the failure modes (impact). I know you're keen on getting to the answer quickly, and your experience is valuable. How did the discussion feel from your side? What could we do so everyone gets to finish their point?"

    Techniques: private setting, specific situation, **observable behavior, not personality** ("you interrupted", not "you're rude"), concrete impact, open question, positive intent stated once.

### Weekly recap (20 items)
1. "Responsible ___ / depend ___ / good ___ / similar ___": fill in. 2. Preposition with *access*? 3. "Discuss about" or "discuss"? 4. *Result in* vs *result from*. 5. "___ Monday ___ 10 a.m.": prepositions. 6. Affect vs effect: which is the verb? 7. Who is the object of *assure*? 8. *Ensure* vs *insure* (US business use)? 9. Give a noun for *assure*. 10. Difference between *impact* and *influence*. 11. Define *behind schedule*. 12. What does *move the needle* mean? 13. *Slip through the cracks*: when do you avoid it? 14. What does RAG mean? 15. What four things does a status update answer? 16. What is SBI? 17. Why describe behavior rather than personality? 18. Which words get stress in a sentence? 19. Stress: *develop* / *development*. 20. Stress shift: *record* noun vs verb.

??? success "Answers"
    1. for / on / at / to. 2. *to*. 3. discuss (no preposition). 4. *result in* = cause; *result from* = be caused by. 5. on / at. 6. affect. 7. A person. 8. Insure = insurance policy; ensure = make certain. 9. assurance. 10. Impact = size of effect; influence = indirect persuasion. 11. Later than planned. 12. Make a measurable difference. 13. In blame-free postmortems; describe the process gap. 14. Red/amber/green status. 15. Where are we, what changed, what is at risk, what do you need. 16. Situation-Behavior-Impact. 17. Behavior is observable and changeable; labels trigger defensiveness. 18. Content words. 19. de-VEL-op / de-VEL-op-ment. 20. RE-cord (noun), re-CORD (verb).

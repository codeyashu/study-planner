# Weekly drills

Twenty-seven weeks (0-26) of 30-minute daily sessions, seven per week. Every week has the same seven-day shape, so you never have to decide what to do: open the page, scroll to today's day, start the timer.

## Day types

| Day | Type | What you do | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Mon) | Grammar | 60-second rule, 10-item exercise, check answers, 3 sentences about your real work | corrected mistakes in the error log |
| 2 (Tue) | Vocabulary | 5 new words (table), collocations, 8-item cloze | 5 rows in the Word table for Anki |
| 3 (Wed) | Speaking | 60-90 s structured answer, pronunciation micro-drill, self-check | one recording (optional) |
| 4 (Thu) | Idioms and phrasal verbs | 5 expressions (table), 8-item exercise, "when not to use them" | 5 rows in the Expression table |
| 5 (Fri) | Writing | rewrite a realistic bad draft under constraints, compare with the model | your rewrite saved |
| 6 (Sat) | Speaking record | 2-minute recorded talk, score with the rubric, shadow a clip | score logged |
| 7 (Sun) | Soft skills and weekly review | scenario, 150-word response, 20-item recap, self-score, error log | top 3 recurring mistakes |

**Timing.** 30 minutes on normal weeks, 20 on light weeks (6, 12, 18, 25, 26; shorter exercises, 3 new words). Use a timer; stop when it rings and note where you got to. Consistency beats intensity: six sessions of 30 minutes beat one of three hours.

**Difficulty ramp.** Weeks 1-8 are medium (B2+ to C1), weeks 9-20 medium-high (C1), weeks 21-26 high (C1+, timed, interview-grade). Week 0 is the baseline: results go in `docs/log/comm-baseline.md` and are repeated in Week 24.

## How to record yourself and score

1. Use your phone voice recorder; one take, no restarts, no notes on Days 3 and 6 (an outline of three keywords is fine).
2. Name files `YYYY-MM-DD-weekNN-dayN.m4a` and keep them in one folder so you can compare Week 0 with Week 24.
3. Listen once for *content* (did I answer first?), once for *delivery* (pauses, stress, fillers). Do not judge accent; judge intelligibility.
4. Score the six-criterion rubric (Clarity, Structure, Fluency, Grammar accuracy, Vocabulary range, Pronunciation; 1-4 each, total /24) and note the two lowest criteria.
5. Add one line to your log: date, total, lowest two criteria, fillers per minute if relevant.

## The error log habit

Keep one plain file (or a note on your phone). Whenever you spot a mistake in your own speech, email or Slack message, or a colleague corrects you, write: **wrong -> right -> rule**. On every Day 7, pick your **top 3 recurring mistakes** and mention them on Monday's grammar warm-up. Weeks 16, 18 and 23 are built around this log, so a rich log makes those weeks far more useful. Recurring mistakes fall into a small number of patterns; naming the pattern is half the cure.

## Vocabulary and Anki

Each Day 2 page has a table with the header `| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |` and each Day 4 page has `| Expression | Meaning | Example |`. The script `scripts/export_vocab.py` reads the **Word** and **Expression** tables from all drill pages and exports them for Anki (front: the word or expression; back: meaning and example). Keep the table headers exactly as they are so the export keeps working. Review the cards for five minutes a day; new words also reappear inside later exercises as spaced repetition.

### Part B — Find and fix the one error (items 31-40)

31. The manager told to me that the release had slipped.
32. Please explain us the pricing model.
33. We have released the hotfix last night.
34. There are too much alerts in this channel.
35. We are responsible of the payments service.
36. I am working here for six years.
37. If the tests will pass, we ship on Monday.
38. The list of open issues are long.
39. She is good in explaining complex topics.
40. The tool lets us to deploy in one click.

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    **Part A**

    1. **b** *until* = continuing up to a point; *by* = no later than.
    2. **c** Present perfect continuous: an activity that started in the past and continues, with a duration (*for six months*).
    3. **b** *Unless* + present simple for a future condition; no *will*.
    4. **b** *insist on* + gerund.
    5. **b** Past perfect for the earlier of two past events.
    6. **b** With *either... or*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject (*developers*).
    7. **c** *Address* is transitive; no preposition.
    8. **b** After a preposition (*to*), the formal relative pronoun for people is *whom*. (*that* cannot follow a preposition.)
    9. **b** *recommend* takes -ing or a *that*-clause ("recommended that we add"). "Recommend us to add" is wrong.
    10. **b** In *Here are...* the verb agrees with the noun that follows (*results*).
    11. **b** Wish about an unreal past = past perfect.
    12. **b** Non-defining clause (commas): *which*, never *that*.
    13. **b** Inversion after *Only after...*.
    14. **b** Passive continuous: the cluster is being migrated.
    15. **b** Future perfect continuous: duration up to a future point.
    16. **b** Reported yes/no question: *whether* or *if*.
    17. **b** Criticism of a past non-action: *should have* + past participle.
    18. **b** *would rather* + subject + past simple for a present/future preference.
    19. **c** *Although* + clause; *despite* needs a noun phrase; "In spite" needs *of*.
    20. **a** *The amount of* + uncountable takes singular; *the number of* takes singular (*has doubled*).
    21. **b** Inverted second conditional (*Were... to*) takes *would* + bare infinitive.
    22. **b** A past-participle clause with passive meaning ("Being left with no context").
    23. **b** Inversion after *Never before*.
    24. **b** *It's about time* + past simple with present/future meaning.
    25. **b** "is believed to have caused" (passive reporting structure, earlier event).
    26. **c** *which* refers to the whole preceding clause.
    27. **b** *Feedback* is uncountable.
    28. **b** *object to* + gerund (*to* is a preposition).
    29. **c** *Equipment* is uncountable.
    30. **b** *No sooner... than* (*hardly... when*).

    **Part B**

    31. The manager **told me** that... (*tell* takes the person directly).
    32. Please explain **the pricing model to us**.
    33. We **released** the hotfix last night (finished time marker: past simple).
    34. There are too **many** alerts (countable).
    35. We are responsible **for** the payments service.
    36. I **have been working** here for six years.
    37. If the tests **pass**, we ship on Monday (no *will* in the if-clause).
    38. The list of open issues **is** long (the head noun is *list*).
    39. She is good **at** explaining complex topics.
    40. The tool lets us **deploy** in one click (*let* + bare infinitive).

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: Final vocabulary check (compare with baseline) {#day-2}
**⏱ 30 min · Tue**

### How to take it
The instrument is the **same 60-word list** as the baseline, because the point is to measure how your rating of the same words changed. To avoid remembered-answer bias, the calibration quiz uses 12 different words. Rate each word honestly:

- **K (Know), 1 point**: you could use it correctly in a sentence right now, with its usual collocations.
- **R (Recognise), 0.5 points**: seen it, roughly know it, would hesitate to use it.
- **N (No), 0 points.**

Time: 12 minutes rating, 8 quiz, 5 idiom-related planning (Day 4), 5 scoring and logging. Rate first, and do not open your baseline log until the quiz is done.

## Day 4 — Idioms & phrasal verbs: Idioms final check {#day-4}
**⏱ 30 min · Thu**

Five new idioms for the final check, then a ten-item recognition check that mirrors the baseline.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| grasp the nettle | To deal firmly and without hesitation with a difficult or unpleasant situation. Neutral to slightly formal; mainly UK. | We finally grasped the nettle and rewrote the billing engine instead of patching it again. |
| the 800-pound gorilla | An obvious, dominant issue or factor that is hard to ignore or address. Informal, mostly US. | The 800-pound gorilla is that we lack the headcount to hit this date. |
| scapegoat (someone) | To unfairly blame one person for something that was not entirely their fault. Neutral to formal; negative. | He scapegoated the intern for an outage that had three underlying causes. |
| get everyone on board | To get people's agreement and active support for a plan. Neutral, common in US and UK. | Before we start, we need to get finance and the carrier team on board. |
| keep your eye on the ball | To stay focused on what matters most. Informal; sports metaphor, universal in business. | With five workstreams in flight, keep your eye on the ball: the December cutover. |

### Exercise
Timer: 12 minutes. Say in your own words what each means; score 1 for a reasonable paraphrase. Then answer 11-14.

1. touch base  2. ballpark (figure)  3. move the needle  4. in the pipeline  5. up in the air  6. meet halfway  7. off the table  8. low-hanging fruit  9. tip of the iceberg  10. raise the bar
11. Choose: *"We're not going to fix this with another patch; it's time to (grasp the nettle / bite the dust / take the bait)."*
12. Rewrite more idiomatically: *"Nobody wants to mention the fact that the project is already failing."*
13. Choose the register: *"They scapegoated me in the review"* is (formal / informal, negative / neutral). Which?
14. Rewrite plainly: *"We need the security team on board before we proceed."*

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. Make brief contact to sync up (informal, business). 2. A rough estimate. 3. Make a noticeable difference to a key metric. 4. Planned or in development, coming soon. 5. Undecided, uncertain. 6. Compromise; each side gives some ground. 7. Not available for discussion or consideration. 8. Easy wins available now. 9. The visible part of a much larger problem. 10. Set a higher standard.

    Score /10 for items 1-10. **8+** = strong; **5-7** = adequate; **below 5** = review the Day 4 tables from earlier weeks.

    11. **grasp the nettle.** *Bite the dust* = fail, die/end; *take the bait* = fall for a lure.
    12. *"Nobody wants to mention the 800-pound gorilla: the project is already failing."*
    13. **Neutral to formal and negative**; it accuses someone of unfair blame. Use carefully even in formal writing.
    14. *"We need approval and active commitment from the security team before we proceed."* Note that *on board* implies commitment, not only permission.

!!! warning "When NOT to use them"
    Do not add idioms to prove fluency: they are best used when they carry meaning faster than a plain phrase. *Scapegoat* and *the 800-pound gorilla* have emotional edges and can sound accusatory in writing to executives. *On board* is informal enough that some non-native colleagues will not know it; check your audience.

# Week 26 — Consolidate and set your next goals

!!! abstract "This week"

    Last week of the communication track. Light week (20 minutes a day). Week 25 attacked your weakest area; Week 26 *consolidates*: you turn what improved into habit and lock in what has not yet moved. Each day gives a short **method** to decide what to consolidate, then **three menus**; do **one** per day. ## Day 1 — Grammar: Free choice: consolidate {#day-1}
**⏱ 20 min · Mon** · 5 min method, 10 min menu (timer), 5 min check and use it.

### Rule in 60 seconds: what to consolidate

1. From your Week 25 error-log prompt, list the errors that **have not moved**. Those are your priority.
2. Test each with a **cold check**: produce a correct sentence about your work in 10 seconds, without preparation. If you fail, it is not yet a habit.
3. Choose the menu by the *kind* of consolidation you need:
    - **Menu A** (timed mixed correction) when errors appear under speed: your slips.
    - **Menu B** (production from real work) when you know the rule but do not produce it spontaneously.
    - **Menu C** (rule teach-back) when you still cannot state the rule; explaining forces precision.
4. **Habit test.** An error is consolidated when you notice it yourself in an email or meeting *before* someone else does. Track how often you catch your own errors; that count is the real measure.

**Exception:** if your Week 24 grammar score was 36 or above, spend the week on style instead (Menu B, sentence variety) rather than accuracy drills.

### Exercise: pick one menu (6 items; timer 10 min)

#### Menu A: mixed timed correction
1. *The number of incidents have dropped since we introducing staged rollouts.* (two errors)
2. *Had we knew about the limit, we would chosen a different design.* (two errors)
3. *She suggested adding a retry, which it was a good idea.*
4. *Not only the deployment failed, but the rollback also didn't work.*
5. *I look forward to see the results of the review.*
6. *The team, that I lead, is responsible for the tracking data.*

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. *The number of incidents **has** dropped since we **introduced** staged rollouts.* *The number of* takes a singular verb; *since* + past simple for the starting event.
    2. *Had we **known** about the limit, we would **have chosen** a different design.* Inverted third conditional: past participle, and *would have* + participle.
    3. *...adding a retry, which **was** a good idea.* *Which* is the subject of the clause; a second pronoun *it* is redundant.
    4. *Not only **did the deployment fail**, but the rollback also didn't work.* Inversion after *Not only*.
    5. *I look forward to **seeing**...* *To* is a preposition.
    6. *The team, **which** I lead, is...* Non-defining clause: *which*, not *that*.

#### Menu B: production from real work
Write six sentences about your own work. Each must use the named structure. Then check with the model column.

| # | Structure | Model (yours will differ) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Narrative tenses (past continuous + past simple) | *We were migrating the booking service when the vendor announced the deprecation.* |
| 2 | Third conditional | *If we had load-tested earlier, we would have caught the throughput problem.* |
| 3 | Passive with a by-phrase omitted | *The connector was delivered three weeks late.* |
| 4 | Participle clause | *Faced with a late connector, we cut scope.* |
| 5 | Inversion | *Rarely have I seen a rollback go so smoothly.* |
| 6 | Cleft (emphasis) | *What worried me most was the missing monitoring.* |

??? success "Checklist for your own sentences"
    A sentence passes if: it is about your real work; the tense matches the time reference; the structure is complete (for example, inversion has an auxiliary before the subject; participle clause subject matches the main clause subject); and you can say it aloud without stumbling. Rewrite any that fail and add the best to `comm-errors.md`.

#### Menu C: rule teach-back (say each aloud in 30 seconds, with one example)
1. *Suggest / recommend*: which patterns follow them?
2. *Present perfect versus past simple*: when do you use each?
3. *Uncountable nouns*: three common in business and how to quantify them.

??? success "Model explanations"
    1. *Suggest and recommend take an -ing form or a that-clause, never to + infinitive.* "I suggest **using** a queue" or "I suggest **that we use** a queue." Wrong: "I suggest to use a queue."
    2. *Past simple for a finished time; present perfect for a link to now or an unfinished period.* "We **released** it on Monday" (finished). "We **have released** it" (result matters now). "We **have worked** on this for six months" (still true).
    3. *Information, advice, feedback, equipment, software, evidence: no plural, no a/an.* Quantify with *some, a lot of, a piece of, an item of, a bit of*: "a piece of advice", "some feedback", "several pieces of evidence".

### Use it
Say your habit-test result: how many of your own errors did you catch this week before anyone else? Write the number in your log.

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: Free choice: consolidate {#day-2}
**⏱ 20 min · Tue** · 5 min method, 5 min table, 8 min menu, 2 min use it.

### Method: spaced consolidation (5 min)
Vocabulary that is not retrieved is lost. Use a Leitner-style plan:

1. Take all Word tables from Weeks 1-25 (or your Anki export).
2. Sort into three boxes by a 10-second recall test (meaning + collocation + a work sentence): **Box 1** fail, **Box 2** partial, **Box 3** solid.
3. Spend 80% of the time on Box 1, and re-test on days 1, 3, 7 and 14, then monthly.
4. **Menu A** if you need meaning recall; **Menu B** if you need context use; **Menu C** if you need active production.

### New words for this week (3 required, 5 given)

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| delineate | verb | To describe or mark out precisely, especially boundaries or responsibilities. Formal. | The RFC delineates which team owns the data and which owns the API. |
| vindicate | verb | To show, after doubt or criticism, that a decision, claim or person was right. Formal. | The postmortem vindicated the on-call engineer's initial diagnosis. |
| reaffirm | verb | To state something again, firmly, to confirm it still holds. Formal-neutral. | Let me reaffirm the constraint: no downtime during peak season. |
| bolster | verb | To support, strengthen or reinforce something. Formal-neutral. | Reliable event delivery bolsters every customer-facing feature. |
| encapsulate | verb | To express the essential nature of something briefly; in engineering, also to enclose behind an interface. | This sentence encapsulates our strategy: fewer systems, clearer ownership. |

### Collocations and word family

- **delineate**: *delineate responsibilities / boundaries / roles / scope*; noun *delineation*. In writing, prefer it for ownership and scope; do not use for simple description.
- **vindicate**: *vindicate a decision / a claim / someone's judgment*; noun *vindication*; adjective *vindicated* ("felt vindicated"). Do not confuse with `vindictive` (seeking revenge).
- **reaffirm**: *reaffirm a commitment / a decision / a policy*; noun *reaffirmation*. Slightly more formal than `repeat`; used to firmly restate something that might be doubted.
- **bolster**: *bolster confidence / an argument / support / defenses*; the participle *bolstered by*. Close synonym of `underpin` (Week 20), but `bolster` suggests strengthening something already standing, while `underpin` suggests forming its base.
- **encapsulate**: *encapsulate the essence / the idea*; noun *encapsulation* (also OOP sense). Verb + object: it is not "to encapsulate about".

## Day 3 — Speaking: Free choice: consolidate {#day-3}
**⏱ 20 min · Wed** · 5 min method, 12 min menu, 3 min self-check.

### Method (5 min)
1. Listen to your **Week 0 baseline** recording (first 60 seconds) and your **Week 25** recording (first 60 seconds). Note three concrete differences: pace, fillers, structure, pronunciation.
2. Choose the consolidation:
    - **Menu A** (re-record an earlier prompt) to lock in the improvement in one criterion.
    - **Menu B** (a 3-minute mini presentation) to test sustained delivery.
    - **Menu C** (tough Q&A) to test performance under pressure.

### Menus (record all takes)

#### Menu A: re-record an earlier prompt
1. Choose the prompt where you scored lowest in Weeks 21-25: STAR (Week 21), design narration (Week 22), career narrative (Week 23).
2. Record it in 90 seconds, applying the one change that worked best in Week 25.
3. Compare the new take against the old on the one criterion you targeted.
4. Record a **second** take without the notes; the second take shows what has become habit.
5. Write down the difference in seconds to the main point and in fillers per minute.
6. Keep the better take as your "benchmark" recording for the next 6 months.

#### Menu B: three-minute mini presentation
1. **Topic:** "One thing our platform should do differently in the next year, and why." (Pick a real one.)
2. **Structure:** point (15 s), problem with a number (30 s), proposal (60 s), trade-off (40 s), ask (25 s), one-line close (10 s).
3. Rehearse once, record once. No slides.
4. Count how many times you looked for a word; note them.
5. Rewrite the three weakest sentences and re-say them.
6. Check the time: 3 minutes plus or minus 15 seconds.

#### Menu C: tough Q&A (30 seconds per answer)
Answer each aloud. Use: acknowledge, answer, evidence, stop.
1. "Why did this take so long?"
2. "What if you're wrong about the root cause?"
3. "Why should we fund this instead of the alternative?"
4. "I don't think that's realistic. Convince me."
5. "What would you do differently?"
6. "What is the biggest risk, and what will you do about it?"

**Pronunciation micro-drill (all menus, 2 min).** Say five times: *"The main risk is the vendor's deadline."* Stress the content words (MAIN RISK, VENdor's DEADline); reduce *the, is, the*. Then the linking: *"risk is"* runs together (ris-kiz).

## Day 4 — Idioms & phrasal verbs: Idiom review round {#day-4}
**⏱ 20 min · Thu** · Five more earlier idioms, chosen for use in negotiation and status talk.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| find common ground | To identify shared interests or points of agreement as a basis for compromise. Neutral, US and UK. | We found common ground: we'll deliver the core in October and the rest in December. |
| a non-starter | An idea or plan that has no realistic chance of being accepted or working. Neutral to informal. | A full rewrite is a non-starter this year; we must refactor incrementally. |
| the path of least resistance | The easiest course of action, requiring the least effort or conflict. Neutral, sometimes mildly critical. | Caching the static lookups is the path of least resistance for latency. |
| a house of cards | A plan or structure that looks fine but is fragile and could collapse if one part fails. Neutral to literary. | The migration plan is a house of cards: if one vendor slips, every date moves. |
| just scratching the surface | Dealing with only a small part of a much larger issue. Neutral, slightly dramatic. | Those three tickets are just scratching the surface; the schema itself is the issue. |

### Exercise (6 items, timer 8 minutes)
1. Choose: *"We can (meet / walk / hit) you halfway on the date."* (Note: `meet halfway`, Week 4, still applies here too — but name the fresh idiom for finding shared interest: find common ______.)
2. Fill: *"A full rewrite is a ______ this quarter."*
3. Rewrite idiomatically: *"These bugs are only the visible part of a much larger issue."*
4. Rewrite idiomatically: *"There are too many separate components to give an exact date."*
5. Choose: *"Let's take the (path of least resistance / path of most resistance / uphill path) and cache the static lookups first."*
6. Which idiom would be poor in a legal or contractual document, and why?

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **ground.** *Find common ground* is the fresh idiom this week; *meet halfway* (Week 4) is a close synonym. 2. **non-starter.** 3. *"These bugs are just scratching the surface."* 4. *"This plan is a house of cards: too fragile to promise an exact date."* 5. **path of least resistance.** 6. *A house of cards*, and most of the others: idioms are imprecise; contracts need literal wording.

!!! warning "When NOT to use them"
    *The path of least resistance* can sound like a criticism ("the lazy option"), so avoid it about someone else's decision in front of them. *A house of cards* signals alarm about fragility; use it only if you have evidence the plan really is that fragile. Do not use two idioms in the same sentence.

## Day 5 — Writing: Free choice: consolidate {#day-5}
**⏱ 20 min · Fri** · 5 min method, 12 min menu, 3 min compare.

### Method (5 min)
Take one real document you wrote in the last month. Run the three-pass edit: (1) **cut** 20-30%; (2) **front-load** the ask or conclusion in the first two lines; (3) **check the six habits** from your error log (articles, prepositions, verb patterns, agreement, tense, register). Pick the menu that matches the pass you find hardest:

- **Menu A** cut hardest? Edit your own paragraph.
- **Menu B** summarize hardest? Write an executive summary.
- **Menu C** peer editing hardest? Edit a colleague's draft with comments.

### Menus (12 minutes)

#### Menu A: cut 30 percent, keep the meaning
**Draft (60 words):** *In order to make sure that we are able to complete the project on time, it is really important that all of the teams involved in the project are able to provide us with their updated estimates for the remaining work by the end of the day on Friday, if at all possible.*

**Task:** cut to 30-35 words.

??? success "Model answer"
    *To finish on time, every team must send us updated estimates for the remaining work by Friday end of day.* (19 words) A version closer to the original register: *Please send updated estimates for your remaining work by Friday, end of day, so we can finish on time.* (19 words)

    **Changes:** "In order to make sure that we are able to" became "To"; "it is really important that" became "must"; "provide us with" became "send us"; the hedge "if at all possible" was cut (add it back only if the deadline truly is flexible).

#### Menu B: executive summary
**Source:** *The tracking platform had three outages in Q3 (37 minutes, 2 h 10 min, 55 minutes), all traced to the same connection-pool setting. We fixed it in October and added an alert. Customer complaints fell 40%. Remaining risk: the failover path has not been tested at peak load. A test needs one engineer for a week and a two-hour window on a weekend in November.*

**Task:** write a 50-word summary with the ask first.

??? success "Model answer"
    *I need approval for a two-hour weekend test window in November and one engineer for a week to test failover at peak load. Three Q3 outages had one cause, a connection-pool setting, now fixed and alerted; complaints fell 40%. Untested failover is the remaining risk.* (46 words)

    **Changes:** the ask and the risk lead; the outage details are compressed to the cause and result; numbers that support the decision are kept (40%, week, two hours).

#### Menu C: edit a colleague's draft
**Colleague's draft:** *Hi team, the deployment tomorrow have been postponed because of the issues that was found in the testing. We will informed you the new date as soon as we know.*

**Task:** correct the errors, and add one comment that helps the writer improve (not just a fix).

??? success "Model answer"
    *Hi team, tomorrow's deployment has been postponed because of issues found in testing. We will let you know the new date as soon as we have it.*

    **Comment:** "Two agreement slips (*has been*, *issues that were*) and one missing form (*will let you know*). Suggestion: add the reason in one line and the next update time (for example, 'by 3 p.m. Thursday') so readers know when to expect news."

    **Changes:** corrected *have > has*, *that was found > found*, *will informed > will let you know / will inform you*; the comment goes beyond corrections and teaches a habit (next-update time).

## Day 7 — Soft skills + weekly review: Set your next 6-month communication goals {#day-7}
**⏱ 20 min · Sun** · 8 min goals, 5 min recap, 4 min self-score, 3 min error log.

### Method: derive goals from your data
Do not set goals from mood; derive them from the four comparison sources you already have:

1. **Baseline vs Week 24 tables**: the areas that improved least.
2. **Error log**: errors that have not moved after two rounds of fixes.
3. **Recordings**: the rubric criterion averaging lowest over Weeks 21-26.
4. **Career plan**: what your next role (Staff/Principal) will demand: writing RFCs, presenting to executives, interviewing others, mentoring.

A good goal is **specific, measurable, dated and observed by someone else** (a colleague, a recording, a document). Limit yourself to **three goals** (one accuracy, one visibility, one relationship).

**Scenario.** It is the last Sunday of the track. Your manager has asked you to send a one-paragraph "communication development plan" for the next six months as part of your Staff-engineer path. **Task:** write 120-150 words with three goals, each with a measure and a date, plus how you will check progress.

### Menu of goal frameworks (pick the one that fits your data)

- **Menu A: accuracy goals.** Example: "Reduce my top-3 errors to under one per 1,000 words in emails, checked monthly by sampling 5 emails." Measure: sampled error counts.
- **Menu B: visibility goals.** Example: "Give one 10-minute talk to another team by March and publish one RFC by April." Measure: delivered, with written feedback.
- **Menu C: relationship goals.** Example: "Hold one candid feedback conversation a month with a peer, using SBI." Measure: a note after each conversation.

??? success "Model response and annotation"
    *Over the next six months I will focus on three goals. First, accuracy: I will bring my three most frequent errors (articles, "suggest to", tense with finished time markers) below one per 1,000 words, measured on five sampled emails a month, starting with the November sample. Second, visibility: I will present a ten-minute design talk to the payments team by January and publish one RFC by March, with written feedback from two reviewers. Third, relationships: I will run one structured feedback conversation a month with a peer or report, using SBI, and note what changed. I will re-record the Week 26 talk in April and compare rubric scores, and I would welcome your view on the plan at our next one-to-one.*

    **Techniques:** three goals only (focus); each has a number or an event and a date (measurable); each has an external observer (feedback, sampling); a scheduled re-test keeps the plan alive; a request for the manager's input turns the plan into a joint one.

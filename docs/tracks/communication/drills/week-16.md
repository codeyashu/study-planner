## Day 1 — Grammar: mixed error review (your personal error log) {#day-1}
**⏱ 30 min · Mon**

### Rule in 60 seconds

Fluent speakers do not fail on grammar they have never seen; they fail on a small set of *fossilized* errors that they repeat under pressure. The fastest gain is to find your own list and drill only that.

**Method (10 minutes):**

1. Open the error logs from weeks 1-15 (your Sunday "top 3" lists) and your recorded talks.
2. Group errors into buckets: **articles**, **prepositions**, **tense/aspect**, **agreement**, **word order in questions/embedded questions**, **verb patterns** (suggest doing, explain to), **uncountables**, **false friends**.
3. Choose your top 3 buckets. Write the correct pattern next to a real sentence of yours.
4. Every day this week, say the three corrected sentences before you start work.

**Ten patterns fluent speakers repeatedly get wrong (check which are yours):**
`since/for` needs present perfect; `discuss/explain/suggest` have fixed patterns; `information/feedback/advice/equipment` are uncountable; embedded questions use statement word order; `if` clauses do not take `would`; `responsible for` (not "of"); `whose` (possession) versus `who's` (who is); `on Monday` (not "in Monday"); "explain something to someone"; "look forward to + -ing".

### Exercise
Correct each sentence (one error each). The items mix patterns from weeks 1-15; your own log takes priority over this list.

1. "I am working on this service since 2019."
2. "We discussed about the migration plan yesterday."
3. "The informations from the vendor are incomplete."
4. "Can you explain me the failure mode?"
5. "If we would have more time, we would test it properly."
6. "She suggested to postpone the release."
7. "Nobody knows where is the config file."
8. "The team is responsible of the outage."
9. "The review is in next Monday."
10. "He is the engineer who's design was approved."

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **"I have been working on this service since 2019."** `Since` with an ongoing situation requires present perfect (continuous), not present simple/continuous.
    2. **"We discussed the migration plan."** `Discuss` is transitive; no `about`. (You can "have a discussion about".)
    3. **"The information from the vendor is incomplete."** Uncountable: no plural, singular verb.
    4. **"Can you explain the failure mode to me?"** `Explain` takes the indirect object with `to`.
    5. **"If we had more time, we would test it properly."** No `would` in the `if` clause.
    6. **"She suggested postponing the release"** or "suggested that we postpone the release." `Suggest` does not take `to + infinitive`.
    7. **"Nobody knows where the config file is."** Embedded questions use statement word order.
    8. **"responsible for"**: fixed preposition.
    9. **"on Monday"** or **"next Monday"** (without a preposition). "In next Monday" is not possible.
    10. **"whose design"**: possessive relative pronoun; `who's` is a contraction of "who is/has".

### Use it
Write your top three personal errors, each with the corrected sentence about your real work, and post them somewhere you see daily (phone lock screen, monitor note). Say the three corrected sentences aloud now.

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: review and 100-word check {#day-2}
**⏱ 30 min · Tue**

### Part A: five new words

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ostensibly | adverb | Apparently or officially, but perhaps not in reality | "The change was ostensibly a performance fix, but it also removed a feature customers relied on." |
| tenable | adjective | Able to be defended or maintained against criticism | "Running two data centers for a single workload is no longer a tenable position." |
| fast-track | verb | To make something happen or be approved faster than the usual process (informal to neutral, common in business English) | "We paid for an express security review to fast-track the release." |
| germane | adjective | Relevant to the matter being discussed; formal | "The only germane finding of the audit is that no access was ever revoked." |
| tacit | adjective | Understood or implied without being stated | "There is a tacit agreement that the on-call engineer owns the rollback." |

Collocations: ostensibly for/to (ostensibly for security reasons); a tenable position / argument / assumption, and its opposite **untenable**; fast-track a request / a shipment / an approval; germane to the discussion / point / question; tacit knowledge / agreement / approval / understanding. Pronunciation: os-TEN-si-bly, TEN-a-ble, FAST-track, jer-MANE, TAS-it.

### Part B: the 100-word check

Below are 100 C1 business words in five blocks of 20. It includes headline words from weeks 7-15 and general C1 vocabulary. For each word give yourself **2** if you can use it in an accurate sentence of your own, **1** if you recognize it but would hesitate to use it, **0** if you do not know it. Maximum 200.

- **Block 1 (analysis):** roughly, approximately, marginally, materially, consequently, whereas, hinge on, compelling, tangible, rationale, anomaly, granular, corroborate, outlier, cohort, substantiate, extrapolate, correlate, skew, baseline.
- **Block 2 (strategy):** leverage, trajectory, headwinds, mandate, remit, accountable, incumbent, differentiator, pivot, scalable, sustainable, viable, feasible, contingency, dependency, prerequisite, milestone, deliverable, constraint, alignment.
- **Block 3 (negotiation and influence):** concession, deadlock, reciprocate, contingent, entrenched, concede, stipulate, leverage (again: use it in a negotiation sentence), stakeholder, buy-in, consensus, compromise, ultimatum, mediate, arbitrate, endorse, advocate, sponsor, dissent, reservations.
- **Block 4 (change and risk):** mitigate, de-risk, unblock, champion, escalate, deprecate, migrate, decommission, remediate, contain, cascade, degrade, regress, exacerbate, precipitate, forestall, avert, pre-empt, safeguard, contingency plan.
- **Block 5 (style and precision):** nuance, ambiguity, concise, verbose, redundant, cohesive, coherent, articulate, elaborate, clarify, reiterate, summarize, paraphrase, caveat, qualify, emphasize, downplay, overstate, underscore, distill.

**Scoring guide**

| Score | Interpretation | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 170-200 | Strong active C1 range | Focus on collocation and register; stop drilling isolated words |
| 130-169 | Solid working range, some passive words | Turn every 1-score word into a sentence about your work |

### Collocations and word family (short list)
- **exacerbate** a problem; **precipitate** a crisis; **forestall** an outage; **remediate** vulnerabilities; **stipulate** in the contract that.
- Word family: tenable / untenable; tacit / tacitly; germane (no common derived forms; near-synonym `relevant`); fast-track (also used as an adjective and noun, `the fast track`); ostensible / ostensibly.

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **ostensibly**: officially claimed, but doubted.
    2. **tenable**: cannot be defended any more.
    3. **fast-track**: speed up the process.
    4. **germane**: relevant to the matter at hand.
    5. **tacit**: unspoken.
    6. **accountable** (week 13): answerable for results.
    7. **corroborate** (week 8): confirm with evidence.
    8. **entrenched** (week 14): firmly established, hard to change.

## Day 3 — Speaking: a 5-minute pitch {#day-3}
**⏱ 30 min · Wed**

**Prompt.** Pitch a platform investment (for example, a shared event-streaming layer) to a VP who has 5 minutes and no technical background. Deliver a two-minute practice version today (the full 5 minutes is Saturday's recording).

**Structure: H-P-S-P-A (Hook, Problem, Solution, Proof, Ask)**

1. **Hook (20 s):** a surprising number or a customer moment: "Last month 1 in 8 booking updates reached the customer more than an hour late."
2. **Problem (40 s):** cost of the current situation in business terms.
3. **Solution (60 s):** what you propose, in one sentence, then how it works in plain language.
4. **Proof (60 s):** evidence: a pilot, benchmark, comparable company.
5. **Ask (20 s):** exact decision and date.

Use the rule of three for emphasis, and pause for two seconds before the ask.

**Pronunciation micro-drill: stress shifts with suffixes**

Stress moves when the suffix changes; practise the sets:

| Word | Related forms |
|---|---|
| e-CON-o-my | eco-NOM-ic, e-CON-o-mist |
| AN-a-lyze | a-NAL-y-sis, an-a-LYT-ic |
| STRAT-e-gy | stra-TE-gic |
| re-spon-si-BIL-i-ty | re-SPON-si-ble |
| or-gan-i-ZA-tion | OR-gan-ize |

Say each row, then a sentence: "The analysis shows the economy of scale is strategic."

**Self-check**
- [ ] The hook was a fact, not "Hi, I'm going to talk about...".
- [ ] Each section had a clear transition ("So what does this mean?", "Here's the proof").
- [ ] I paused before the number and before the ask.
- [ ] I stayed inside the time limit without speeding up.
- [ ] My suffix-stress words were correct (eco-NOM-ic, a-NAL-y-sis).

## Day 4 — Idioms & phrasal verbs: idiom review round {#day-4}
**⏱ 30 min · Thu**

New idioms (five), then a review mix.

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| on the back burner | Postponed; low priority for now. Informal, neutral tone. | "The logging cleanup is on the back burner until Q1." |
| bite the bullet | To do something unpleasant that cannot be avoided, decisively. Informal; common in the US and UK. | "We bit the bullet and rewrote the auth module." |
| a double-edged sword | Something with both benefits and drawbacks. Neutral; suitable in formal writing. | "Microservices are a double-edged sword: more autonomy, more operational overhead." |
| cut corners | To skip steps to save time or money, usually reducing quality. Negative; never say of yourself in a good light. | "We can't cut corners on the security review." |
| the elephant in the room | An obvious problem everyone avoids mentioning. Informal but acceptable in meetings. | "Let's address the elephant in the room: the timeline is unrealistic." |

### Exercise (8 items)

1. Choose: "Automatic retries are ______; they hide errors but reduce user-visible failures." (a double-edged sword / on the back burner)
2. Fill in: "We've delayed the migration too long; it's time to ______."
3. Fill in: "Skipping load tests to hit the date means ______ on quality."
4. Fill in: "Nobody mentioned the budget cut, the ______ in the room."
5. Review: which phrasal verb (week 9) means to formally approve? (roll out / sign off / scale up)
6. Review: "This outage is just the ______" (week 15 idiom).
7. Review: "Please ______ the security team in the thread" (week 11 phrasal verb: loop in / flag up).
8. Rewrite more idiomatically: "We decided to postpone the redesign and give it low priority."

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **a double-edged sword**: benefit and drawback.
    2. **bite the bullet**: do the unavoidable unpleasant thing.
    3. **cutting corners** (present participle).
    4. **elephant** ("the elephant in the room").
    5. **sign off**: formal approval; roll out is release, scale up is increase capacity.
    6. **tip of the iceberg** (week 15).
    7. **loop in** (week 11): include someone in communication; "flag up" means to draw attention to an issue.
    8. "We put the redesign **on the back burner**."

**When NOT to use them:** "elephant in the room" can feel confrontational if said about a specific person's decision; name the topic instead ("Can we discuss the timeline risk?"). Avoid *cut corners* in customer-facing text, as it implies negligence. In formal proposals prefer "postponed", "unavoidable decision", "trade-off".

## Day 5 — Writing: the one-pager {#day-5}
**⏱ 30 min · Fri**

**Bad draft** (for a director who will decide in 2 minutes):

> Subject: Observability
>
> We have been thinking a lot about observability and how we do it now is not really working well. There are many tools and teams use different ones, which causes confusion and sometimes people can't find logs during incidents. It would probably be good to consolidate somehow. I've looked at a few tools and they all have pros and cons. We should discuss.

**Task.** Rewrite as a one-pager (around 180-220 words) with these headings: **Recommendation, Why now, Options, Risks, Ask**. It must be skimmable in two minutes, with numbers (invent plausible ones), one option rejected with a reason, and one named risk with mitigation.

??? success "Model answer"
    **One-pager: Consolidate observability on one platform by Q2**

    **Recommendation.** Standardize on a single observability platform (logs, metrics, traces) by end of Q2 and retire the other four tools.

    **Why now.** During incidents, engineers spend a median 17 minutes locating the right logs across five tools, and 3 of the last 5 sev-1 incidents lasted longer because of it. Licensing across tools costs about 620k dollars a year, of which roughly 200k is duplicated capability.

    **Options.**
    1. *Consolidate on Platform A (recommended):* covers all three signals, existing integration with our deployment pipeline; migration effort about 10 engineer-weeks.
    2. *Keep the status quo:* no migration cost, but the incident-time penalty remains.
    3. *Build in-house:* rejected; ongoing maintenance cost exceeds the license saving within 18 months.

    **Risks.** Migration could disrupt alerting. Mitigation: run both platforms in parallel for six weeks and cut over per service.

    **Ask.** Approve Platform A and 10 engineer-weeks by 15 October.

    What changed and why:
    - Headings and order (recommendation first) make it skimmable; the director can decide after the first paragraph.
    - "Confusion" became a measured cost (17 minutes, 3 of 5 incidents, 200k duplication).
    - "Somehow" and "pros and cons" became named options with a rejection reason.
    - One risk with a mitigation shows you have thought beyond the pitch.
    - "We should discuss" became a dated ask.

## Day 7 — Soft skills + weekly review: executive presence {#day-7}
**⏱ 30 min · Sun**

**Scenario.** In a leadership review, the CFO interrupts your slide: "Your cost estimate feels 30 percent too low. Why should I trust this?" Eight senior people are watching. Your estimate is sound but based on one assumption you haven't mentioned.

**Task.** Script your reply in about 150 words: composed tone, answer first, evidence, and honest handling of the assumption. Do not become defensive.

??? success "Model response"
    [Pause for a beat; lower your pitch.] "That's a fair challenge, and I'd want to be able to defend it. Short answer: I believe the estimate is sound, and it depends on one assumption I should have put on the slide.

    Here's the basis. We built the figure from actual costs on the last two comparable migrations: 14 and 16 engineer-weeks respectively. The assumption is that we reuse the existing payments adapter. If we have to rebuild it, add roughly 5 weeks, which brings the estimate closer to your number.

    So the range is 15 to 20 weeks, and the low end is contingent on the adapter. I'll confirm that with the payments team by Thursday and send you the revised number. Does that address your concern?"

    **Techniques used:** (1) A pause and lower pitch before answering signals calm. (2) Validate the challenge without conceding the point ("fair challenge"). (3) Answer first, then evidence. (4) Disclose the assumption yourself before it is exposed; this builds credibility. (5) Turn a single number into a range. (6) Commit to a follow-up date. (7) Close with a check question to return control.

## Day 1 — Grammar: Grammar diagnostic quiz (40 items) {#day-1}
**⏱ 30 min · Mon**

### How to take it
Set a 25-minute timer. Answer items 1-30 by choosing a, b, c or d. Items 31-40 each contain exactly one error: rewrite the sentence correctly. No dictionary, no AI, no search. Guess if unsure, but mark guesses with a `?` so you can see how much of your score is luck.

### Part A — Choose the correct option (items 1-30)

1. We need to make a decision ___ end of the week. a) by the  b) until the  c) in  d) at
2. She has been working on the migration ___ March. a) for  b) since  c) during  d) from
3. If the API ___ down, the failover starts automatically. a) will go  b) goes  c) would go  d) went
4. I'm not used to ___ on call every other week. a) work  b) working  c) worked  d) be working
5. The team ___ the release by the time the client called. a) already finished  b) has already finished  c) had already finished  d) was already finish
6. Neither the manager nor the engineers ___ aware of the change. a) was  b) were  c) is  d) has been
7. We discussed ___ the new pricing model in the meeting. a) about  b) on  c) to  d) (no preposition)
8. This is the engineer ___ code caused the outage. a) who  b) whose  c) which  d) whom
9. He suggested ___ the deployment until Monday. a) to delay  b) delaying  c) us delay  d) that delaying
10. There ___ several issues with the proposal. a) is  b) are  c) has  d) have
11. I wish I ___ more time to prepare for yesterday's review. a) have  b) had  c) had had  d) would have
12. The report, ___ was published last week, contains errors. a) that  b) which  c) what  d) who
13. Not only ___ the deadline, but they also exceeded the budget. a) they missed  b) did they miss  c) they did miss  d) missed they
14. The servers ___ at the moment, so please try again later. a) are upgrading  b) are being upgraded  c) upgrade  d) have upgraded
15. By next year, we ___ the platform for five years. a) will maintain  b) will have been maintaining  c) are maintaining  d) maintained
16. She asked me ___ I had reviewed the pull request. a) that  b) if  c) what  d) does
17. You ___ me; I would have helped. a) should call  b) should have called  c) must have called  d) had to call
18. I'd rather you ___ the customer before sending the fix. a) tell  b) told  c) to tell  d) telling
19. ___ the traffic, the truck arrived on time. a) Although  b) Despite  c) However  d) Even though of
20. The number of incidents ___ dropped since January, but a number of teams ___ still affected. a) has / are  b) have / is  c) has / is  d) have / are
21. Had we known about the limit, we ___ a different design. a) would choose  b) would have chosen  c) will choose  d) had chosen
22. ___ by three consecutive failures, the team rolled back. a) Alarming  b) Alarmed  c) Having alarmed  d) To alarm
23. Rarely ___ such a clean design. a) I have seen  b) have I seen  c) I saw  d) did I seen
24. It's high time we ___ the legacy service. a) retire  b) retired  c) will retire  d) are retiring
25. He is said ___ the best architect in the company. a) being  b) to be  c) that is  d) be
26. The proposal was rejected, ___ surprised no one. a) that  b) what  c) which  d) it
27. Which sentence is correct? a) The informations are in the wiki.  b) The information is in the wiki.  c) The informations is in the wiki.  d) An information is in the wiki.
28. Which sentence is correct? a) I look forward to hear from you.  b) I look forward to hearing from you.  c) I look forward to have heard from you.  d) I look forward hearing from you.
29. Which sentence is correct? a) The CEO gave us an advice.  b) The CEO gave us some advices.  c) The CEO gave us a piece of advice.  d) The CEO gave us an advices.
30. Hardly had the deployment started ___ the alarms went off. a) than  b) when  c) then  d) so

### Part B — Find and fix the one error (items 31-40)

31. We are agree with the proposal.
32. She explained me the process.
33. I have seen him yesterday at the stand-up.
34. The team has less engineers than last year.
35. Despite of the delay, we shipped on time.
36. He is working here since 2019.
37. If I will have time, I will review your PR.
38. Each of the services have its own database.
39. I'm interested to work on the platform team.
40. We must to finish the audit before Friday.

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    **Part A**

    1. **a** "by the end of the week": *by* = no later than; *until* describes a state continuing up to a time.
    2. **b** *since* + starting point; *for* + duration ("for three months").
    3. **b** Zero/first conditional: present simple in the if-clause for a general or likely condition.
    4. **b** "be used to" + -ing (a gerund, because *to* is a preposition here).
    5. **c** Past perfect for the earlier of two past events.
    6. **b** With *neither... nor*, the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("engineers").
    7. **d** *Discuss* is transitive: no preposition. "Discuss about" is a very common error.
    8. **b** *whose* = possessive relative ("the engineer's code").
    9. **b** *Suggest* takes -ing or "that + clause" ("suggested that we delay"), never "to + verb".
    10. **b** Existential *there* agrees with the noun that follows ("issues").
    11. **c** Wish about an unreal past = past perfect.
    12. **b** Non-defining relative clause (commas) cannot use *that*.
    13. **b** Negative adverbial at the start (*Not only*) triggers inversion (auxiliary before subject).
    14. **b** Passive continuous: an action in progress, done to the servers.
    15. **b** Future perfect continuous: duration up to a future point.
    16. **b** Reported yes/no question uses *if/whether*.
    17. **b** Criticism of a past non-action: *should have* + past participle.
    18. **b** *would rather* + subject + past simple for a present/future preference.
    19. **b** *Despite* + noun phrase; *although* needs a clause; "despite of" does not exist.
    20. **a** *The number of* takes a singular verb; *a number of* means "several" and takes a plural.
    21. **b** Inverted third conditional (*Had we known*) needs *would have* + past participle.
    22. **b** Past participle clause with a passive meaning ("having been alarmed"). "Alarming" would mean the team alarmed others.
    23. **b** Inversion after *rarely*.
    24. **b** *It's high time* + past simple, with present meaning.
    25. **b** "is said to be" (passive reporting structure).
    26. **c** *which* refers to the whole preceding clause; *that* is not used in non-defining clauses.
    27. **b** *Information* is uncountable: no plural, no *a/an*, singular verb.
    28. **b** *look forward to* + gerund (*to* is a preposition).
    29. **c** *Advice* is uncountable; use "a piece of advice".
    30. **b** *Hardly... when* (also *No sooner... than*). *Hardly... than* is a mixed-up pair.

    **Part B**

    31. We **agree** with the proposal. (*Agree* is a verb, not an adjective: no "be".)
    32. She explained **the process to me**. (*Explain* takes the thing directly, the person with *to*.)
    33. I **saw** him yesterday. (A finished time marker like *yesterday* needs past simple, not present perfect.)
    34. The team has **fewer** engineers. (*Fewer* with countable plural nouns.)
    35. **Despite** the delay / **In spite of** the delay. (*Despite of* does not exist.)
    36. He **has been working** here since 2019. (*Since* needs a perfect tense.)
    37. If I **have** time, I will review your PR. (No *will* in the if-clause.)
    38. Each of the services **has** its own database. (*Each* is singular.)
    39. I'm interested **in working** on the platform team. (*interested in* + gerund.)
    40. We must finish the audit. (Modal verbs take the bare infinitive, no *to*.)

### Scoring guide

Give 1 point per correct item. A correct fix in Part B counts only if the whole sentence is now correct.

| Score /40 | Approximate level | What to prioritise |
|---|---|---|
| 36-40 | Strong C1 or above | Precision and style: weeks 9-20 (inversion, rhetoric, nominalisation). Treat weeks 1-8 as fast review. |
| 30-35 | C1 | Follow the plan as written. Pay extra attention to your specific misses. |
| 22-29 | B2+ | Weeks 1-8 are essential, especially articles, prepositions, tenses. Do the Day 1 exercise twice (once cold, once after 3 days). |
| 14-21 | B2 | Consider adding one extra 15-minute grammar session per week; focus on items 1-20 topics. |
| under 14 | B1+ | Grammar first: repeat Week 1-4 Day 1 drills slowly before advancing; use the topic pages linked from the [track index](../index.md). |

**Diagnose by topic**: count misses by cluster and write them in the baseline log.

| Cluster | Items |
|---|---|
| Tenses and aspect | 2, 5, 14, 15, 33, 36 |
| Conditionals and wishes | 3, 11, 17, 18, 21, 24, 37 |

| Agreement and countability | 6, 10, 20, 27, 29, 34, 38 |

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: Vocabulary level check (60 words) {#day-2}
**⏱ 30 min · Tue**

### How to take it
Below are 60 words in three bands of 20. Rate each word honestly:

- **K (Know), 1 point**: you could use it correctly in a sentence right now, and you know its usual collocations.
- **R (Recognise), 0.5 points**: you have seen it and roughly know the meaning, but would hesitate to use it.
- **N (No), 0 points**.

Then take the 12-item verification quiz to calibrate your ratings. Time: 12 minutes for ratings, 8 for the quiz, 5 for the idiom check, 5 for scoring and logging.

### Band A (upper-intermediate, B2)
drawback · feasible · thorough · oversight · workaround · tedious · tentative · viable · straightforward · elaborate (verb) · inevitable · reluctant · adequate · prone · substantial · outweigh · undermine · endorse · cumbersome · foresee

### Band B (advanced, C1)
scrutiny · pragmatic · nuanced · ambiguous · tenuous · exacerbate · alleviate · foster · cumulative · discrepancy · inherent · plausible · contentious · culminate · entrench · discretion · fragmented · reconcile · disparity · precarious

### Band C (high advanced, C1+/C2)
ubiquitous · expedite · obviate · tacit · salient · conducive · egregious · tenable · insidious · unwieldy · circumvent · ostensibly · cursory · incongruous · vindicate · capitulate · proliferate · attrition · fungible · orthogonal

### Verification quiz: match the word to its meaning
Words: **workaround, tentative, oversight, exacerbate, tenuous, discrepancy, plausible, alleviate, ostensibly, obviate, cursory, egregious**

A. a mistake caused by failing to notice something  
B. hasty and not thorough  
C. a way of avoiding a problem without fixing its cause  
D. outrageously bad, in a way that stands out  
E. to make a problem worse  
F. to reduce the severity of something unpleasant  
G. weak or slight (a link, a connection)  
H. to remove a need or difficulty in advance, so that something is unnecessary  
I. reasonable and believable, though not proven  
J. not certain or final; provisional  
K. apparently, but perhaps not in reality  
L. a difference between things that should be the same

??? success "Answers and scoring"
    **Quiz key:** workaround C · tentative J · oversight A · exacerbate E · tenuous G · discrepancy L · plausible I · alleviate F · ostensibly K · obviate H · cursory B · egregious D

    **Scoring**
    1. Vocabulary score = (number of K) + 0.5 x (number of R). Maximum 60.
    2. Quiz score /12. If the quiz score is below 8, your K ratings were optimistic. Halve your K count in Band C and recompute.

    | Adjusted score /60 | Level indication | Action |
    |---|---|---|
    | 48-60 | C1+ | Your receptive range is wide. Work on active use: the Day 2 "Use it" step is the priority. |
    | 36-47 | C1 | Follow the plan; aim for the Week 8, 16, 20 vocabulary checks. |
    | 24-35 | B2+ | Focus on Band B words; add 5 minutes of reading per day (business news, engineering blogs). |
    | under 24 | B2 | Learn Band A/B misses first; use the Anki export of each week's Word table. |

    Also count Band scores separately: a big drop between A and B shows where your range thins out.

## Day 3 — Speaking: Baseline speaking prompts {#day-3}
**⏱ 30 min · Wed**

Record yourself (phone voice memo is fine) answering these three prompts, **60-90 seconds each, one take each, no notes**. Do not restart if you stumble: hesitations are exactly what we want to measure.

1. **Self-introduction.** Introduce yourself to a new leadership team: your background, what you work on, and what you want to be known for in the next two years.
2. **A technical decision.** Describe a recent technical decision you made or influenced: the options, what you chose, and why.
3. **An incident.** Describe a production incident you handled: what happened, how you responded, and what changed afterwards.

### Structure (natural, not scripted)
Use whatever structure comes naturally. Afterward you will notice whether you had one. In later weeks you will learn PREP and pyramid structures.

### Pronunciation baseline (5 min)
Read these aloud and record them. Do not look up stress first.

- Stress: **de-VEL-op-ment, en-GI-neer, ar-chi-TEC-ture, ANN-a-lyze, a-NAL-y-sis, com-MIT-tee, e-CO-nom-y, PRO-cess (n), pro-CESS (formal v.), re-COM-mend, ma-NAGE-ment, IN-fra-struc-ture, a-VAIL-a-bil-i-ty, vul-ner-a-BIL-i-ty**
- Minimal pairs: ship / sheep, full / fool, live / leave, sit / seat, thirteen / thirty, fifteen / fifty, world / word
- Sentence: "We deployed the fix at fifteen minutes past thirty, but only fifty of the thirteen hundred users were affected."

??? success "How to review the recording"
    Listen once with only one question: **where did I hesitate, and why?** Common reasons: searching for a word, restructuring a sentence mid-flow, worrying about grammar. Note 3 examples. Also note your approximate speed: count the words in 30 seconds of transcript; comfortable clear speech is roughly 130-150 words per minute.

## Day 5 — Writing: Write a 150-word status email (baseline) {#day-5}
**⏱ 30 min · Fri**

### Task
Write a status email of **about 150 words** (140-170 is fine). Timebox 20 minutes, then a 10-minute self-review. Do not use a grammar checker until step 3.

**Scenario.** You lead the platform team at a global logistics company. Your migration of the booking service to a new event-streaming platform was due to finish on 30 October. A third-party vendor delivered its connector three weeks late; testing showed a throughput problem at peak load. You now expect completion on 27 November. The cost impact is small but customer-facing risk exists for the December peak season. You need a decision from your VP by Friday: extend the timeline, or launch on 30 October with a reduced feature set.

Write the email to the VP (Priya Nair) and copy the product manager.

### Self-review (10 min)
1. Read it aloud once. Mark any sentence where you had to breathe in the middle of a clause.
2. Highlight in one color the sentence that states the decision you need. Where is it? (Top, middle, bottom?) 
3. Count the average sentence length. Over 25 words is a warning sign.
4. Circle any article ("a/the") you were unsure about, any preposition you guessed.
5. Run a grammar checker and compare: what did it catch that you missed?

### Scoring (1-4, self-assessment)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose is clear early | not stated | in the last lines | stated, but after context | stated in the first two lines |
| Concision | rambling | some padding | mostly tight | every sentence earns its place |
| Precision (dates, numbers, ask) | vague | some detail | most details specific | dates, numbers and ask all explicit |
| Accuracy (grammar, articles, prepositions) | frequent errors | several errors | 1-2 errors | error-free |
| Tone (professional, direct) | too casual/too stiff | inconsistent | mostly appropriate | natural and confident |

Log your total /20 and paste the email in `docs/log/comm-baseline.md`. In Week 24 you write the **same** email again and compare.

## Day 6 — Speaking record: Record a 2-minute baseline talk and score it {#day-6}
**⏱ 30 min · Sat**

### Task
Record a **2-minute** talk (5 minutes to plan, 2 to record, 15 to score, 8 to shadow).

**Prompt:** "Describe the most significant technical challenge you have led. What was the situation, what did you do, what was the outcome, and what did you learn?"

### Suggested structure
Situation (20 s) -> Your actions (60 s) -> Result with numbers (20 s) -> Lesson (20 s). If you use a different structure, note it.

### Scoring rubric (score each 1-4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | listener must work hard to follow | main point recoverable | clear with minor confusion | effortless to follow |
| Structure | no discernible order | partial order | clear beginning and end | clear signposting throughout |
| Fluency | frequent long pauses and restarts | noticeable hesitations | occasional hesitation | smooth, natural rhythm |
| Grammar accuracy | errors block meaning | frequent errors, meaning clear | occasional errors | rare errors |
| Vocabulary range | repeats basic words | some variety | good range, some imprecision | precise, varied, natural collocations |
| Pronunciation | frequently unintelligible | intelligible with effort | mostly clear | clear stress, rhythm and intonation |

Total /24. Compare with the anchors: 6-10 = developing, 11-15 = working proficiency, 16-20 = strong, 21-24 = near-native fluency for professional settings.

### Shadowing task (8 min)
Choose a 60-90 second clip from a conference talk or TED talk by an engineer or leader you admire (ted.com), or a BBC Learning English "6 Minute English" episode (bbc.co.uk/learningenglish). Play one sentence, pause, repeat it copying rhythm and intonation exactly. Do this for 8-10 sentences. It is baseline: just notice which sentences you cannot copy.

### Log your score
Add to `docs/log/comm-baseline.md`: date, total /24, the two lowest criteria, and the recording file name.

## Day 7 — Soft skills + weekly review: Communication self-assessment {#day-7}
**⏱ 30 min · Sun**

### Scenario and task
Rate yourself on each statement, **1 = rarely true, 2 = sometimes, 3 = usually, 4 = almost always**. Think about the last three months of real meetings, not your best day.

**Speaking and listening**

1. I can explain a technical topic to a non-technical stakeholder without losing them.
2. I speak at a pace others can follow, with pauses at natural points.
3. I rarely use filler words (um, you know, like).
4. I ask clarifying questions before responding to a complex point.
5. I can summarise what someone said accurately before I disagree.
6. I feel comfortable speaking up early in a meeting.

**Structure and clarity**

7. My answers begin with the main point, then support it.
8. I can give a 3-minute briefing without notes.
9. I use signposting language ("There are three reasons...", "To sum up...").
10. I keep my emails short, with the ask in the first two lines.
11. I can cut my own drafts by 30 percent without losing meaning.
12. My documents are easy to skim (headings, bullets, front-loaded summary).

**Language accuracy and range**

13. I rarely worry about articles and prepositions when writing.
14. I choose precise verbs rather than "do", "make", "get".
15. I can hedge appropriately ("This might suggest...") when uncertain.
16. I use idioms and phrasal verbs naturally and know when not to.
17. I can adjust my register between a team chat and an executive email.
18. I notice my recurring errors and correct them.

**Influence and relationships**

19. I can disagree without damaging the relationship.
20. I give feedback that is specific and actionable.
21. I receive feedback without becoming defensive.
22. I can say no while offering an alternative.
23. I can de-escalate a tense discussion.
24. I adapt to communication styles from different cultures.

**Executive communication**

25. I can give a concise executive update with a clear recommendation.
26. I handle tough questions calmly and honestly.
27. I can tell a compelling story about my work.
28. I can facilitate a meeting so decisions get made.
29. I feel confident in high-stakes interviews and negotiations.
30. I would be comfortable presenting to a room of senior leaders tomorrow.

??? success "Scoring and interpretation"
    Sum your ratings (30-120). Compute the average for each block.

    | Total | Interpretation |
    |---|---|
    | 100-120 | Very confident; check for blind spots by asking a colleague to rate you too. |
    | 80-99 | Solid; a few clear development areas. |
    | 60-79 | Significant room to grow; the plan targets exactly these areas. |
    | under 60 | Prioritise the two lowest blocks; ask for feedback from someone you trust. |

    The lowest block is your **priority soft-skill theme**. Note that self-ratings tend to be biased: experienced engineers often overrate structure and underrate listening. The most informative move: ask one trusted colleague to rate you on items 4, 7, 19, 21 and 25 and compare.

## Day 1 — Grammar: Present perfect vs past simple, and time expressions {#day-1}
**⏱ 30 min · Mon**

### Rule in 60 seconds
- **Past simple** = an action or state that is *finished* and located at a *finished time*: "We **deployed** the fix yesterday." Time markers: *yesterday, last week, in 2023, two days ago, when, at 3 a.m.* Questions with *when* or *what time* always take past simple.
- **Present perfect** (have/has + past participle) = a past event *connected to now*: (1) a result that matters now ("The pipeline **has failed**, so nothing is deploying"); (2) experience up to now ("I **have led** three migrations"); (3) a period that is *not finished* ("**this week**, **so far**, **since 2021**, **for six years**"). Common markers: *already, yet, just, ever, never, recently, lately, so far.*
- **Present perfect continuous** stresses duration or an activity with visible results: "We **have been migrating** for months." Stative verbs (know, be, have, want) do not take the continuous: "I **have known** him since 2015."
- **Unfinished period, careful:** *this morning* is present perfect only while it is still morning; after that use past simple.
- **The error fluent speakers make:** using present perfect with a finished time ("I **have seen** him yesterday"), using present simple with *since/for* ("I **work** here since 2019"), and asking "When **have** you joined?" American English accepts past simple with *already/yet/just* in speech ("Did you eat yet?"), but present perfect is safer in writing and in British usage.

### Exercise
Choose the correct form or fix the error.

1. We (deployed / have deployed) the fix at 3 a.m.
2. I (worked / have worked) on this platform since 2021.
3. When (did you join / have you joined) the team?
4. Fix: Last quarter we have reduced infrastructure costs by 12 percent.
5. Fix: We are waiting for the vendor since Monday.
6. This week we (resolved / have resolved) three incidents. (It is Thursday.)
7. We (resolved / have resolved) three incidents last week.
8. Fix: How long do you work on this system?
9. Fix: I know the product manager since 2015.
10. The team (has been migrating / was migrating) services for months, and it is nearly done.

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. **deployed** (specific finished time: 3 a.m.). 2. **have worked** (*since* + unfinished period). 3. **did you join** (*when* + past simple). 4. ...we **reduced** costs by 12 percent. (*Last quarter* is finished.) 5. ...we **have been waiting** for the vendor since Monday. 6. **have resolved** (*this week* is unfinished). 7. **resolved** (*last week* is finished). 8. How long **have you worked / have you been working** on this system? 9. I **have known** the product manager since 2015. (*Know* is stative: no continuous.) 10. **has been migrating** (duration to now, with the current relevance "nearly done").

### Use it
Say three true sentences: one with *since*, one with *yesterday* or *last week*, one with *so far this month*. Notice which tense each requires.

## Day 2 — Vocabulary: Tech-leadership verbs (champion, unblock, de-risk) {#day-2}
**⏱ 30 min · Tue**

| Word | Part of speech | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| champion | verb / noun | verb: to actively support and advocate for an idea or initiative; noun: the person who does so | She championed the move to contract testing across four teams. |
| unblock | verb | to remove an obstacle that prevents someone or something from progressing (informal, common in engineering) | I spent the morning unblocking the mobile team by fixing the API mock. |
| de-risk | verb | to reduce the uncertainty or risk of a project, often through early experiments or staged delivery | We de-risked the rewrite by migrating one low-traffic service first. |
| socialize | verb | in business US usage: to circulate an idea informally to build support before a formal decision | Let's socialize the proposal with the platform leads before the review. |
| codify | verb | to put practices or rules into a clear, written, systematic form | The incident channel etiquette was never written down; we codified it in a runbook. |

### Collocations and word family
- **champion** an initiative / a cause / a change; *a champion* (of quality), *an internal champion*, *executive champion*; do not confuse with *sponsor* (provides budget or cover).
- **unblock** a team / a colleague / a release / a pipeline; opposite *block*; noun *blocker*; phrasal *clear the way*.
- **de-risk** a project / a launch / an investment; often *early*, *cheaply*; also written *derisk*. Noun: *derisking*.
- **socialize** an idea / a proposal / a plan; *socialize with* also means to mix with people, so context matters. Register: US corporate; some readers find it jargon.
- **codify** a policy / standards / best practices / a process; noun *codification*.

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. de-risk. 2. socialize. 3. championed. 4. codified. 5. unblock. 6. champion (noun). 7. quantify. 8. substantiate.

## Day 3 — Speaking: Intonation for emphasis and questions {#day-3}
**⏱ 30 min · Wed**

### Prompt (60-90 seconds)
"Describe a recent surprise on a project. Ask two rhetorical or direct questions in your answer and make one point strongly."

### Structure
Setup -> Surprise (emphasized) -> Question to the audience -> What you did.

### Pronunciation micro-drill
- **Falling tune** (pitch drops on the last stressed syllable): statements, commands, and wh-questions. "We shipped it **YEST**erday. ↘ What **WENT** wrong? ↘"
- **Rising tune**: yes/no questions and checking. "Did the tests **PASS**? ↗" "So we're **DE**ploying on Friday? ↗"
- **Fall-rise** (↘↗): "yes, but..." or partial agreement or hesitancy. "The design is **FINE**, ↘↗ (but I have concerns)." It signals something is coming.
- **Emphasis = the nuclear stress.** The most important word carries the biggest pitch movement and is louder and longer. Move the stress to change the meaning:
    - "**I** didn't say the migration failed." (someone else said it)
    - "I didn't **SAY** the migration failed." (I implied it)
    - "I didn't say the **MIGRATION** failed." (something else did)
- **Lists:** rising on each item, falling on the last: "latency ↗, throughput ↗, and **COST** ↘."
- Drill: read each sentence three times moving the nuclear stress to different words, and say what each version implies.

### Self-check
- [ ] My yes/no questions rose and my wh-questions fell.
- [ ] I put the biggest pitch movement on the key word, not the last word automatically.
- [ ] My list intonation signalled continuation (rise) and completion (fall).
- [ ] I did not use a flat tone throughout.

## Day 4 — Idioms & phrasal verbs: Risk and uncertainty: in the pipeline, up in the air {#day-4}
**⏱ 30 min · Thu**

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| in the pipeline | planned or in development, not yet delivered; neutral, business | We have two new integrations in the pipeline for next quarter. |
| up in the air | undecided, uncertain; informal | The launch date is still up in the air until legal signs off. |
| in the works | being prepared or developed; informal, US | A rate-limiting feature is in the works. |
| touch and go | uncertain, with a real chance of failure; informal, more UK but understood in the US | It was touch and go whether we'd finish before the freeze. |
| a long shot | an attempt unlikely to succeed; informal | Getting the vendor to fix this by Friday is a long shot. |

### Exercise
1. "The budget decision is still ___ the air."
2. "Two hires are in the ___ for Q4." (pipeline / cracks / snag)
3. Which idiom means "unlikely to succeed but worth trying"?
4. Rewrite: "Whether we can restore service in time is genuinely uncertain, with real risk of failure."
5. Rewrite plainly: "It's in the works."
6. True/false: *in the pipeline* is inappropriate in formal business writing.
7. Choose the best: "The result is ___ until we see the load test." (a long shot / up in the air / in the pipeline)
8. Which two expressions describe things being prepared?

??? success "Answers and explanations"
    1. up in the air. 2. pipeline. 3. a long shot. 4. "It was touch and go whether we could restore service in time." 5. "It's being developed / prepared." 6. False: *in the pipeline* is neutral; *in the works* and *up in the air* are informal. 7. up in the air (undecided pending information). 8. in the pipeline, in the works.

!!! warning "When NOT to use them"
    In risk registers and contracts, use precise language: "probability: 30 percent; decision date: 15 Oct". Do not use *touch and go* in customer-facing incident updates: it sounds alarming and vague. Avoid *pipeline* when you also mean a CI/CD pipeline in the same paragraph: ambiguity.

## Day 5 — Writing: Meeting notes and action items {#day-5}
**⏱ 30 min · Fri**

Good notes: **decisions, actions (owner + deadline), open questions**, and a two-line summary. Notes are not a transcript.

### Bad draft
> Meeting notes: We talked about the release. John said there might be a problem with the database and someone should look at it. Sarah thinks we should maybe wait. Then we discussed the vendor and how the contract is going. Also there was a discussion about the new dashboard and how it should work. People were generally in agreement. We'll meet again soon.

### Task
Rewrite as structured notes in under 120 words. Invent plausible details: two decisions, three action items (owner + date), one open question.

??? success "Model answer and what changed"
    > **Release sync, 2 Oct.** Attendees: John, Sarah, Priya, Rahul.
    > **Summary:** Release 4.2 is on hold pending a database check; vendor contract review continues.
    >
    > **Decisions**
    > 1. Hold release 4.2 until the migration script is verified (Sarah, Priya).
    > 2. Build the dashboard in phases, starting with tracking status.
    >
    > **Actions**
    > - John: run migration script on staging by Mon 6 Oct.
    > - Sarah: confirm rollback plan by Tue 7 Oct.
    > - Rahul: send vendor contract questions to Legal by Fri 3 Oct.
    >
    > **Open question:** Can the vendor deliver the connector by 20 Oct? (Owner: Priya; answer due 8 Oct.)
    > **Next meeting:** Thu 9 Oct.

    - Summary first (2 lines).
    - Vague "someone should look" became an owner and a date.
    - Removed opinions attributed loosely; recorded decisions instead.
    - Open questions have owners.
    - Fixed next-meeting date replaces "soon".

## Day 6 — Speaking record: Intonation for emphasis and questions (2-minute recording) {#day-6}
**⏱ 30 min · Sat**

### Task
Record 2 minutes. **Prompt:** "Explain why a decision your team made was right, and address one likely objection with a question and a response." Use varied intonation: falling for statements, rising for checking, emphasis on key words.

### Structure
Decision (20 s) -> Reasons (50 s) -> Objection as a question (20 s) -> Response (30 s).

### Scoring rubric (1-4)

| Criterion | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | hard to follow | main idea recoverable | clear | effortless |
| Structure | no order | partial | clear start and end | signposted |
| Fluency | frequent long pauses | noticeable hesitations | occasional | smooth |
| Grammar accuracy | blocks meaning | frequent errors | occasional | rare |
| Vocabulary range | basic | some variety | good | precise, varied |
| Pronunciation | unintelligible in places | needs effort | mostly clear | clear stress, rhythm, intonation |

### Shadowing task
Shadow one minute of an expressive speaker: a TED talk (ted.com) or a BBC Learning English episode (bbc.co.uk/learningenglish). Mark pitch peaks on the transcript and copy them.

**Log your score:** date, total /24, weakest criterion, whether your intonation felt flat (yes/no).

## Day 7 — Soft skills + weekly review: Receiving feedback without defensiveness {#day-7}
**⏱ 30 min · Sun**

### Scenario
After your architecture presentation, the VP of Product, Elena, says: "Honestly, I found the deck too technical. I didn't get what decision you wanted from me." You feel it was unfair: you had a whole slide on the decision.

### Task
Write a 150-word response (or script) that: acknowledges, asks for one concrete example, states what you will change, and does not defend or over-apologize.

??? success "Model response and techniques"
    > "Thank you, Elena. That's useful to hear. I want to make sure I understand: was it the level of detail, or that the decision wasn't clear early enough? Could you point to one place where I lost you?
    >
    > (After her answer.) That makes sense. I put the decision on slide nine, and it should have been the first thing you saw. In the next version I'll open with a one-line recommendation and the decision I need, then keep the technical detail in an appendix. May I send you a revised version by Wednesday and get your reaction?"

    Techniques: **thank without agreeing to everything**; **clarify before reacting**; **separate impact from intent**; specific commitment and date; no justification ("but I had a slide...").

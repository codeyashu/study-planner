# Communication & English track

!!! abstract "Track overview"
    **Level:** B2+ heading to C1 (medium-to-high difficulty, no beginner material) · **Rhythm:** one 30-minute drill a day, 7 days a week (20 minutes on light weeks) · **Span:** week 0 baseline, weeks 1-24, buffer weeks 25-26 · **Audience:** a senior engineer moving to Staff/Principal in a global logistics enterprise.

## Goals
1. **Precision:** write and speak with fewer grammar and collocation errors, especially the ones fluent non-native speakers keep (articles, prepositions, tenses, uncountables).
2. **Range:** a working vocabulary of C1 business and tech-leadership words, idioms and phrasal verbs, used with the right register.
3. **Fluency:** speak calmly and clearly on calls, in presentations and in interviews: deliberate pace, few fillers, structured answers.
4. **Impact:** write emails, updates, RFCs and executive summaries that get action; handle feedback, negotiation, Q&A and cross-cultural situations like a Staff engineer.

## The four areas
| Area | What you train | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Grammar and precision | Articles, prepositions, tenses, modals and hedging, sentence structure, clause linking, punctuation, common errors | Topic pages below; Monday drills |
| Vocabulary, idioms, phrasal verbs | Business and tech vocabulary, nuance and register, idioms, phrasal verbs, collocations with data | Tuesday and Thursday drills |
| Spoken fluency and pronunciation | Stress, rhythm, intonation, pace and pauses, spoken structure, meetings, shadowing | Wednesday and Saturday drills |
| Business writing and soft skills | Concise writing, email, design docs, executive updates; feedback, negotiation, storytelling, executive presence, listening | Friday and Sunday drills |

## How the daily 30-minute drills work
Each week has a page in [Weekly drills](drills/week-00.md) with seven days. Open the page, go to the day heading, set a timer. The focus of each day matches the weekly arc.

| Day | Type | What you do in 30 minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Grammar | 60-second rule, 10 exercise items (error correction, transformation), then say or write 3 sentences about your real work |
| Tue | Vocabulary | 5 new words in a table, collocations, an 8-sentence cloze, 3 sentences of your own |
| Wed | Speaking | A 60-90 second work prompt with a structure, a pronunciation micro-drill, a 5-point self-check |
| Thu | Idioms and phrasal verbs | 5 expressions with register notes, an 8-item exercise, a warning about when not to use them |
| Fri | Writing | Rewrite a realistic bad draft under constraints, compare with the model answer and the edits |
| Sat | Speaking record | A 2-minute recorded talk, scoring rubric (1-4 on six criteria), a shadowing task, log the score |
| Sun | Soft skills and review | A scenario and a 150-word response or script, then a 20-item weekly recap and an error-log prompt |

Light weeks (6, 12, 18, 25, 26) use 20 minutes: about 6 exercise items and 3 new words, same headings. Difficulty ramps: weeks 1-8 medium, 9-20 medium-high, 21-26 high and timed.

Habits that make it work:
- Do the exercise before you open the answers; explain your choice aloud, then check.
- Keep an **error log** (a note file; each error, the correct form, the rule). The weekly review and weeks 16, 18, 23 use it.
- Record yourself at least twice a week. Listening to your own voice is the fastest feedback you have.
- Use new words in real Slack messages, emails and meetings within 48 hours.

## Baseline and checkpoints
**Week 0** is the baseline: a 40-item grammar diagnostic, a 60-word vocabulary check, a recorded talk, a 150-word status email, and a 30-item self-assessment. Record the results in `docs/log/comm-baseline.md`. Checkpoints repeat the measures at weeks 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24 (checkpoints comm-1 to comm-6 in the plan) and week 24 compares with the baseline directly.

| Checkpoint | Week | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | 0 | Full diagnostics; save scores |
| comm-1 | 4 | Re-score the speaking rubric on a fresh 2-minute talk; count errors in a 150-word email |
| comm-2 | 8 | 100-word vocabulary check; 5-minute presentation recorded and scored |
| comm-3 | 12 | Grammar mini-test (20 items from your error log); executive summary rewrite |
| comm-4 | 16 | 5-minute pitch; error-log review: which errors are gone, which remain |
| comm-5 | 20 | 10-minute technical talk with Q&A; vocabulary check |
| comm-6 | 24 | Repeat all baseline tests; compare |

**How to score**
- **Grammar:** items correct out of 40 (or 20 for the mini-test). Track by category (articles, prepositions, tenses, modals, structure), not just the total.
- **Vocabulary:** words known and usable in a sentence (not just recognised) out of 60 or 100, by band.
- **Speaking:** the 1-4 rubric from the Saturday drill on six criteria (clarity, structure, fluency, grammar accuracy, vocabulary range, pronunciation), total out of 24. Also measure: words per minute (aim 130-150), fillers per minute (aim under 2), longest pause.
- **Writing:** for the same email or paragraph, count word count, errors per 100 words, and whether the ask and the date are in the first two lines.
- **Soft skills:** the 30-item self-assessment (1-4) plus feedback from one trusted colleague.
- **Trend rule:** improvement of 10-15 percent per quarter in errors per 100 words and fillers per minute is realistic. Do not chase a total score; fix your top three errors.

## Sound like a Staff engineer: one-page phrase sheet
Print or keep open. Use each phrase once this week, in real speech.

**Open with the point**
- "My recommendation is X. There are three reasons."
- "I would like a decision on X by Friday."
- "The short version: it will slip by two weeks."

**Give a view with the right strength**
| Strong | Moderate | Tentative |
|---|---|---|
| "I am confident that..." | "I would recommend..." | "My initial view is..." |
| "The data clearly shows..." | "It appears that..." | "It seems possible that..." |
| "We must not..." | "We should avoid..." | "It might be worth avoiding..." |

**Disagree well**
- "I see it differently, and here is why."
- "I have a concern about the rollback path."
- "Can we test that assumption before we commit?"
- "I disagree, and I will commit to the decision."

**Ask and clarify**
- "Help me understand what is driving the date."
- "When you say real time, what latency do you mean?"
- "What am I missing?"
- "Let me play that back to check I have it."

**Buy time / recover**
- "That is a good question; let me think for a second."
- "I do not have that number here; I will send it by end of day."
- "Let me rephrase that." "Let me correct that."

**Say no**
- "I am not able to take that on this quarter."
- "Yes, if we drop X."
- "Not now. Can we revisit in March?"

**Trade-offs and options**
- "There are two options. Option A is faster, but it adds risk. Option B is..."
- "The trade-off is cost versus latency."
- "If we do nothing, X happens by Q3."

**Precision with data**
- "Roughly 40 percent; the range is 35 to 45."
- "Up from 12 to 19 percent, a rise of seven points (about 60 percent)."
- "Marginal", "material", "significant": use them for what they mean.

**Escalate and update**
- "Status: amber. Cause: X. Ask: Y. Next update: Thursday 10:00 CET."
- "I am raising this early because..."
- "Decisions needed from you: one."

**Close**
- "To summarise: recommendation, risk, next step."
- "Owners and dates: A by Tuesday, B by Friday. Please tell me if I have missed anything."

**Avoid** (these weaken or confuse): "I just wanted to..." "Sorry, quick question" "I will try" (for a real no) "Does that make sense?" (repeated) "As per my last email" "Kindly revert" (in a global audience, prefer "Could you reply by...?").
